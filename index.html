What is given, what is not

Not given: organisms, boundaries, goals, a reading frame. Every earlier code-soup (Tierra, Avida, the 2024 BFF soup) hands life a container — a fixed tape per creature. Here there is only one field; where a body starts and stops has to emerge.

Given, honestly: the executor. A spark reads a straight ray, works for reach steps, and a spark that copied productively begets a child where it wrote and fires again in place. That inheritance of execution is stipulated — without it a copy is never run, and nothing lives.

The lens

For each cell: T, how fast its matter is being overwritten; P, of the cells just overwritten nearby, what share still carry the old form — in the best-matching frame, corrected for luck. Living = T·P. Front = T·(1−P). It knows nothing about this world: on Conway's Life it reads a glider as 1.00 living, a still life as inert, chaos as 0.14.

It was wrong twice on the way. It called a copy-disabled control 28% alive (one + in a still patch looked like persistence), then called Life's chaos 79% alive (searching 27 frames finds lucky matches in a binary alphabet). Both caught by controls, both fixed.

What the soup taught

My hand-written replicator went extinct — it overwrote its own bracket. The first thing that ever took over was one I never designed: [ L0 , ], where each spark smears a single byte and only the population of sparks copies a row. Replication as a collective act.

Eight scheduling dials gave only mud or monoculture. The missing quantity was reach. In-place arithmetic turned out to be the mutation operator: switch it off and the world collapses to seven symbols. Second-order selves were hunted by pointing the same lens at a coarse map of aliveness: not found. Coherence beat a time-shuffled null only slightly (0.54 vs 0.47, three seeds) — what slow first-order bodies alone would give. Left alone for 12,000 epochs the world settles near 6% living, in islands, inside a sea of front.
